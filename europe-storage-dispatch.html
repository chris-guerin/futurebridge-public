FutureBridge Signals · Storage & dispatch · August 2026

Europe’s storage shortfall is already parked outside. The question is who gets to dispatch it.

The EU needs 200 GW of storage by 2030 and holds about 55 today. Its own medium scenario reaches roughly 160 GW.1 The gap is real, it is late, and there is no procurement mechanism attached to it.

Meanwhile a second storage estate is assembling itself on driveways and in car parks, paid for by consumers, at a scale that dwarfs the shortfall. Europe increasingly does not manufacture it. And the layer that decides what it is worth — the right to dispatch it — is being settled now, quietly, by commercial deals rather than policy.

01The question

Not who owns the batteries. Who dispatches them.

The instinctive worry about Chinese vehicles in Europe is ownership, and it is the wrong worry. The electricity never leaves. A battery in a Birmingham driveway charges from the British grid and discharges into it, whoever built the cell.

What can move is the economics, the data and the control. A flexibility asset earns when someone registers it in a market, forecasts it, and dispatches it. That party sets the split, sees the household’s consumption, and owns the relationship. Everything upstream — cell, vehicle, charger, finance — is a supply contract.

The electrons stay British. The margin, the data and the control need not.

So the useful question is not how many EV batteries become available to the system. It is who will hold the commercial rights to Europe’s mobile storage estate when it matters. Section 02 is the stack that question resolves into.

03The doors

Nobody needs to build an Octopus. The ingredients are for sale.

The reflexive question is whether a Chinese-owned Octopus equivalent exists in Britain. It does not — the Ofgem licensee register shows no Chinese-controlled domestic supplier and aggregator. That is the point. Building one is slow. Acquiring the parts is not, and three doors are demonstrably open.

04The ingredients

BYD is already a European energy company

Not a carmaker with energy ambitions. Count what is already built.9

Two ingredients are missing, and Tesla has just shown one of them takes eight months. That is the whole distance between “BYD sells cars in Britain” and BYD Energy UK.

Worth noting how deliberately China is pacing this at home. The NDRC has stood up 30 large-scale V2G pilots across nine cities, targeting 5,000 bidirectional charging facilities by end-2027 — within an estate of 28 million charging points.11 That is 0.02%. China is learning vehicle-to-grid slowly and at small scale domestically, while exporting the capability at volume. Whether that ordering is caution or sequencing is the interesting question.

No BYD electricity supply licence or own aggregator was found in any European market at the time of writing. Recorded as absent, not as intent.

05The incumbents

What the utilities have, and whether it is enough

Aggregated flexibility capacity, where a figure is publicly sourced.

Is it enough? Against today’s market, comfortably. Against the estate arriving in section 06, no. Kraken is the largest residential VPP in the world at 2 GW across 500,000 devices.5 A European passenger EV parc of 40 million cars, on conservative availability assumptions, is an order of magnitude larger than every VPP listed above combined.

The incumbents are not losing a race they are running. They are being outgrown by an asset class none of them manufactures — and most are positioned as buyers of that flexibility, not owners of it. EDF bought the charging estate. E.ON partnered with an OEM. Shell owns sonnen. Each is a claim on one layer.

For most European utilities this is a spectator sport with a ticket price.

And the flow runs both ways, which is the part the alarmed version of this argument misses. In January 2026 Octopus formed Bitong Energy with China’s PCG Power, taking Kraken into Chinese power trading with an ambition of 140 TWh a year by 2030 — roughly the UK’s entire current green output — and about £50m of annual profit, half of it returning to Britain.6 Britain is acquiring a dispatch position in China at the same moment China is acquiring a supply position in Britain. This is a trade in layers, not a raid.

06The arithmetic

The shortfall, and the estate that dwarfs it

The EU needs 200 GW of storage by 2030 against about 55 GW today, and its own medium scenario lands near 160 GW — a shortfall around 40 GW. Twenty-two member states have committed only 30–35 GW of new capacity by 2028, and the plan carries no procurement mechanism, no auction framework and no mandatory obligation.1 Britain sits inside the same physics: about 7.5 GW installed, a Clean Power 2030 target of 23–27 GW, and a connections queue of 221 GW — roughly three times what the target implies.7

Model, not forecast. EU passenger EV parc taken as ~40 million by 20308 growing to ~68 million by 2035, stationary storage on the ~160 GW medium path. Mobile capacity = parc × capable share × availability × per-vehicle power. It measures power at a moment, not energy over a day, and assumes contracted access that does not yet exist for most of the parc. The point is the order of magnitude, not the decimal.

On mid-range assumptions the mobile estate is not a supplement to the stationary target. It is larger than the entire shortfall, and it arrives without a procurement mechanism, without an auction, and without anyone having decided who may dispatch it.

Europe is about to miss a target it could meet with assets it has already paid for — and does not control.

That is the forcing function. Not ownership, not tariffs, not sovereignty rhetoric. A concrete gap between what the grid needs and what the plan delivers, closable by an estate that is privately owned, mostly foreign-manufactured, and contractually unattached. Whoever attaches it first sets the terms.

07What to watch

Six observable thresholds

Written so the evidence can settle them.

Licence
A Chinese-owned entity obtains a GB or EU electricity supply licence

The single move that shifts the bottom three layers of the stack. Tesla’s eight-month path is the clock.

Estate
A Chinese buyer acquires a UK or EU charge point operator

The distressed window is open now — EO into administration, Mer sold, consolidation to five or six expected. EDF has already shown the move works.

Parc
Chinese-built cars exceed 30% of new V2G-capable EV registrations in any large European market

Drives the red band in section 06. Britain’s only live retail V2G product is already a Chinese car.

Aggregation
Any OEM-owned platform dispatches more than 1 GW of third-party vehicles in Europe

The test of whether carmakers can hold the flexibility layer at all, or remain suppliers into someone else’s book.

Policy
The EU or UK attaches a dispatch-access condition to vehicle or charger type approval

The obvious policy response to section 06, and nobody has proposed it. Its absence is the story; its arrival would be the turn.

Counter-flow
Bitong Energy reaches material traded volume in Guangdong

Tests whether the layer trade is genuinely two-way, or whether European software exports while European hardware share falls.
