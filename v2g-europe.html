FutureBridge Signal Engine · Vehicle-to-grid · August 2026

Europe just fixed the regulation. The cars are now the constraint.

For a decade the vehicle-to-grid business case died on the same point: the same electron was charged grid fees twice. Germany removed that in November 2025.1 From 1 January 2027 every newly installed or refurbished charge point in the EU must support the standard that makes bidirectional power transfer possible.6

That is the regulatory question closing. It leaves a harder one open. The infrastructure becomes V2G-capable by mandate; the vehicle parc does not. And the parc is what the EU’s take-up figures are counted against.

01The barrier

It was never the technology

Bidirectional hardware has worked since the CHAdeMO Leaf. What did not work was the arithmetic. In most European markets electricity drawn into a car battery paid network charges, and the same electricity paid again on the way back out. On a round trip that is a levy on an asset doing the grid a service.

On 13 November 2025 the Bundestag amended the Energy Industry Act (EnWG) to end double network charging and to treat electricity fed back from an EV battery as storage electricity.1 The Bundesnetzagentur’s MiSpeL determination gives it operational form from 1 October 2026, with a transitional period to 30 September 2027 during which it applies only by agreement with the grid and metering operator.23

The economics did not improve. The penalty was removed.

That distinction matters for anyone sizing this market. Nothing about the value V2G creates changed in November 2025. What changed is that a structural tax on capturing it was lifted — in one member state, on a timetable that runs into late 2027 before it is unconditional.

03The models

Four commercial models, four different owners of the customer

The technology is common to all of them. What differs is who holds the billing relationship, and therefore who captures the flexibility margin.

Model 01 · Retailer-led

The energy supplier owns it

The tariff is the product. The car is an input to a portfolio the supplier already optimises. Highest margin capture, because the supplier nets V2G against its own imbalance position rather than selling a service to a third party.

Octopus Power Pack, UK — reported saving around £620 a year against a standard variable tariff, with Octopus’ own modelling up to £850.4
Captured by: the retailer. The OEM supplies a compliant vehicle and sees none of the margin.
Model 02 · OEM-led

The carmaker owns it

Bidirectional charging sold as a vehicle feature with a captive wallbox and an energy partner behind the scenes. Defends the OEM’s relationship with the driver and creates a post-sale revenue line, at the cost of building an energy business.

Renault 5 with the PowerBox Verso in France — first commercially available V2G offer worldwide, October 2024, quoted at up to €600 a year.5 Volkswagen with Elli, and Mercedes-Benz on the electric GLC and CLA from 2026.5
Captured by: the OEM — if it can carry the energy-market obligations. Most cannot alone.
Model 03 · Paired

OEM plus utility, split economics

The pragmatic middle. The carmaker brings the parc and the warranty position; the utility brings market access, metering and settlement. Fastest route to a live offer, and the model most European launches have actually taken.

BMW with E.ON since February 2026 — wallbox, tariff and smart meter as one package, with a bonus quoted up to €720 a year.5 Ford with Octopus from summer 2026; Kia and Hyundai in the Netherlands from late 2025.5
Captured by: split. Whoever owns the tariff tends to own the renewal.
Model 04 · Fleet and depot

Aggregation without a consumer

Predictable dwell time, one counterparty, no consumer churn. The only model where V2G economics work today without a retail tariff, because the operator can commit capacity with confidence.

Utrecht’s car-sharing deployment — 50 Renault 5s from June 2025, scaling toward 500. The Johan Cruijff ArenA has run since 2019.5 Nuvve’s revenue remains driven by V2G school-bus hardware rather than by traded flexibility.7
Captured by: the fleet operator or aggregator. The clearest near-term P&L.
04Competition

“Pan-European” is a platform claim, not a market position

Electricity markets are national. Balancing products, network tariffs, metering rules, settlement periods and consumer protection all stop at the border. A V2G proposition that works in Rotterdam does not port to Dortmund without re-plumbing, and the German rules do not become unconditional until late 2027.2

So the pan-European players are not competing for a European market. They are competing to amortise one platform across many national markets faster than a national incumbent can build its own. Three postures are visible:

The vertically integrated retailer. Octopus is the clearest case — it owns the customer, the tariff, the optimisation stack and increasingly the OEM partnerships, and it exports the stack. Its constraint is that it must win retail energy customers country by country.

The neutral aggregator. The Mobility House, Jedlix, Virta, Nuvve. They sell the market-access layer to whoever owns the customer. Scales across borders more easily precisely because it does not need the retail relationship — and captures less per vehicle for the same reason.

The utility incumbent. E.ON, Enel X and their peers, moving through OEM partnerships in their home territories. Strong where they already hold the meter; weak where they do not.

Nobody is winning Europe. Several are winning the right to be the layer everyone else rents.

05Take-up

When does this map to the EU’s figures?

The reference point most often used is that 30% of EVs are V2G-capable by 2030, the figure in the Coalition of the Willing on Bidirectional Charging.8 Europe’s passenger EV parc is projected to pass 40 million by 2030.9 Thirty per cent of that is roughly twelve million cars able to export power.

Set against that: of 146 EV models scheduled for release in 2024, four supported ISO 15118-20 Plug&Charge.10 Transport & Environment’s proposal is for a voluntary “V2G-ready” label now and mandatory type approval only by 203211 — two years after the date the 30% figure is set against.

06Thresholds

What would prove this wrong

Written as claims with thresholds, so the evidence can break them.

Threshold · breaks if false
The binding constraint moves from regulation to the vehicle parc during 2027

Falsified if, by the end of 2027, more than three major EU markets still charge network fees on exported EV electricity. Germany’s transitional regime running to 30 September 2027 is the thing to watch.2

Threshold · breaks if false
V2G-capable share of new EU EV sales exceeds 25% by 2029

The AFIR charge-point mandate from 1 January 20276 creates the socket. It does not create the car. Falsified if OEM model announcements through 2027–28 keep bidirectional confined to flagship trims.

Threshold · breaks if false
Retail tariff models out-earn neutral aggregation per vehicle by 2028

If the neutral aggregators match retailer economics per vehicle, the vertical integration thesis is wrong and the market layers rather than consolidates. Watch the per-vehicle figures quoted in successive Octopus, E.ON and Mobility House offers.

Threshold · breaks if false
Fleet and depot V2G remains the only profitable segment through 2027

Falsified by a retail V2G proposition reaching six figures of connected vehicles in any single European market. Nuvve’s revenue mix — still hardware-led rather than flexibility-led — is the current counter-evidence.7
